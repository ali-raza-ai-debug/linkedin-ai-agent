Ever wondered why we need two types of regression in Machine Learning?

When I first started studying ML, Linear and Logistic regression sounded almost identical. 

Drafting models got much easier once I understood their core difference.

Here is how I break them down in plain words.

Linear Regression predicts continuous numerical values. 
Companies use it to forecast metrics like house prices, salaries, or sales revenue. It gives you an exact number.

Logistic Regression predicts categorical, binary outcomes. 
It helps classify things into two categories, like checking if an email is spam or not spam, or evaluating loan approvals.

So, how does Logistic Regression turn data into a simple yes-or-no choice?

It uses an S-shaped sigmoid function that acts like a dimmer switch. 
This transforms outputs into a probability score strictly between 0 and 1.

To make the final call, it checks if the score crosses a decision threshold. 
This threshold is typically set at 0.5 (or 50%). If the probability crosses 0.5, the model predicts a "yes."

In short: Linear gives you a continuous number, while Logistic gives you a binary choice.
