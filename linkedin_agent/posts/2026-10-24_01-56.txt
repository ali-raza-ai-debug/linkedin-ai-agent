Why do we hide data from our own machine learning models?

When I started learning ML, it felt strange to hold back good data.

Why not train the model on everything we have?

Because if a model sees all the data during training, it can simply memorize the answers.

It gets a high score on paper, but fails completely in the real world. This is called overfitting.

This is not just a theoretical problem. 

During the COVID-19 pandemic, systematic reviews of over 400 AI tools showed that almost none were fit for actual clinical use.

A major reason was flawed training methods and hidden data leaks between training and evaluation data. 

To prevent this, we split our data into train and test sets. 

The training set is like the textbook. The test set is the final exam with completely unseen data. 🤖

Testing on unseen data exposes overfitting and prevents fake accuracy scores before we deploy a model.

It keeps us honest about what our model actually knows!
