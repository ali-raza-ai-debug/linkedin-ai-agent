How do computers actually see an image? 🖼️

When I started studying deep learning, I wondered how a machine could make sense of raw pixels.

Today, as part of my learning journey, I want to share a simple look at Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs).

To a computer, an image is just a grid of numbers. 

CNNs are specialized deep learning models designed specifically to process this grid-like data, making them the foundation for modern computer vision.

Instead of looking at everything all at once, CNNs use small filters (also called kernels).

These filters slide over the data to automatically learn and capture spatial patterns and visual features step by step.

Because of how effectively they learn these features, CNNs have become the standard approach for real-world image recognition operations. 🤖

It is fascinating to see how math turns simple numbers into visual understanding.
