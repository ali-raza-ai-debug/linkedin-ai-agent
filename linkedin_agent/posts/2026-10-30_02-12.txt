Why make one decision when you can ask a whole crowd?

As I continue my machine learning journey, decision trees and random forests quickly became two of my favorite concepts to study.

Think of a single decision tree like a simple flowchart.

Should I carry an umbrella today?
Is it cloudy? Yes.
Is it windy? Yes.
Decision: Take the umbrella.

They are intuitive and easy to interpret.

However, a single tree can sometimes be too sensitive to specific training data.

That is where a Random Forest steps in. 🌲

Instead of relying on just one tree, it builds a multitude of individual decision trees during training.

When it needs to make a classification, every single tree casts a vote, and the forest picks the prediction that wins the majority vote.

It is literally the wisdom of the crowd applied to algorithms.

A fun real-world example: Microsoft actually used randomized decision forests in the Xbox Kinect sensor to analyze depth images and classify human body parts in real time for gaming.

It is amazing to see how combining simple trees can solve such complex, real-time problems.
