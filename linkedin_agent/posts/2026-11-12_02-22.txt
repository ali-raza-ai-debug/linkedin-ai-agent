Ever wondered how ChatGPT actually writes full paragraphs so fast?

As I continue my journey of learning LLMs, I realized the core secret is surprisingly simple.

It all comes down to next-token prediction.

Think of an LLM as a superpowered auto-complete loop. 🤖

The model does not write whole sentences at once. 

Instead, it guesses the single best next token, appends it to your prompt, and repeats this process over and over to build fluent text.

So, what is a token? 

It is not always a full word. A token can be a complete word like "cat", a fragment like "un" or "ing", or even a single punctuation mark.

As a quick rule of thumb:
One token equals roughly 4 characters, or about 0.75 English words. That means a standard paragraph takes around 100 tokens.

Understanding this loop also helped me see why models hallucinate.

Because LLMs generate text by predicting probable next tokens rather than retrieving facts, fabrications tend to happen around precise details like exact names, dates, and statistics. 💡

It is fascinating how predicting one token at a time can create such natural conversations!
