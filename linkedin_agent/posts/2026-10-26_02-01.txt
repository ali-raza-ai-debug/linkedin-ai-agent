Would you trust a student who got the exact exam questions before the test?

That is exactly why we split our data into training and testing sets in machine learning. 💡

When I started learning ML, I wondered why we deliberately hide a portion of our data during training.

It comes down to evaluating how the model performs on unseen information.

Here is why this simple step is so important:

1. Prevents deceptively high accuracy
Without a proper split, models suffer from data leakage. This gives us a false sense of success with high metrics that crumble upon actual deployment.

2. Simulates real-world conditions
Keeping test data completely isolated ensures we evaluate the model on information that would not be available at prediction time in production.

In high-stakes fields like medical AI, improper splitting can conceal data leakage. A model might report stellar benchmark results, but completely fail on new patient datasets. 🧠

Splitting data keeps our models honest before they ever reach production.
