Imagine giving a student the exact exam questions before test day.

They would easily get a perfect score.

But did they actually learn the concepts? Or did they just memorize the answers?

This is the exact reason why we split our data in Machine Learning.

Without a train-test split, a model can memorize dataset-specific quirks and noise rather than real patterns. It gives a false appearance of high accuracy, but fails in the real world.

I like to think about it through a simple exam analogy:

1. Training set: Practice questions used to learn the material.

2. Validation set: Mock exams used for tuning without peeking at the final test.

3. Test set: The final proctored exam to check real performance on unseen data.

Using a structured ratio like a 70/20/10 split keeps these stages strictly separate. It prevents data bleed and shows if the model is truly ready for live deployment.

It is a simple step, but essential for building reliable AI models!
