Why do older AI models forget what was said at the beginning of a long text?

As I continue my Deep Learning journey, I have been looking at Recurrent Neural Networks, or RNNs.

They were a big step forward for processing text.

But they have a major weakness with long sentences.

When processing text, an RNN unrolls into a time step for every single word.

If a text is a few hundred words long, the network becomes extremely deep.

And this creates a big problem during training.

Signals have to travel back through all those steps.

This leads to exponential memory loss.

The gradients collapse, and the network simply forgets the early context in the passage.

Researchers actually figured out this math way back in 1994, known as the vanishing gradient problem.

Plus, because RNNs process text sequentially one token at a time, it creates a massive bottleneck.

This exact limit is what eventually pushed the AI world toward Transformers.
