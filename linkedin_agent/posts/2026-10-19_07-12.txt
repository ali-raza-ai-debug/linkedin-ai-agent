Have you ever built a machine learning model that scored 100 percent on practice data, but failed completely in the real world?

That is the trap of overfitting.

It happens when a model learns the random quirks and fine noise of its training data rather than the underlying rules.

Instead of actually learning, it just memorizes the answers.

We see this risk everywhere in the real world.

For example, diagnostic chest X-ray models can fall victim to shortcut learning.

The AI might memorize superficial details like image textures or hospital scanner tags instead of identifying genuine biological markers of illness.

Because it memorized the training room instead of the disease, it struggles when tested in a new hospital environment.

So how do we fix this?

We make sure our models see diverse data and we test them on things they have never seen before.

As I continue learning AI, understanding these hidden traps is just as exciting as building the models themselves.
